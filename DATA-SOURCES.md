# Jissr: data sources, licences and personal data

The organisers provide no data. Jissr uses **only material we created or have the legal right to use**, and **no real personal data**.

## 1. Data used in the prototype

| What | Source | Rights / licence |
|---|---|---|
| 38 patient records (30 diabetes, 8 bowel screening), 9 staff members, all case histories | **Invented by the team** and generated by code (`core/data.mjs`). Dates are computed relative to "today". | Our own work. No real person is represented; any resemblance of a name to a real person is coincidental. |
| Clinical thresholds: prediabetes 5.7–6.4 %, diabetes range ≥ 6.5 % | Published clinical guidance: American Diabetes Association, *Standards of Care in Diabetes* (classification and diagnosis); WHO (2011), *Use of Glycated Haemoglobin (HbA1c) in the Diagnosis of Diabetes Mellitus*. | Facts used as thresholds; no text copied. |
| Bowel screening (FIT): positive result leads to colonoscopy; 30-day deadline and messages | Written and **proposed by the team**. No LOINC code is claimed for FIT (to be confirmed). | Our own work. Awaiting clinician review. |
| "Urgent" ≥ 10 %, all service levels, deadlines and escalation rules | **Our own proposals**, not taken from any guideline. | Our own work. Awaiting clinician review. |
| Patient messages in English and Arabic | Written by the team (`core/templates.mjs`). | Our own work. Arabic awaiting native-speaker review. |
| Emergency number 998 (UAE ambulance) | Public information. | Public fact. |
| HbA1c code in the FHIR export (LOINC 4548-4) | LOINC. | Used under the LOINC licence: "This material contains content from LOINC (http://loinc.org). LOINC is copyright © Regenstrief Institute, Inc. and the Logical Observation Identifiers Names and Codes (LOINC) Committee and is available at no cost under the license at http://loinc.org/license." |
| FHIR R4 resource structure | HL7 FHIR standard. | Open standard (published by HL7 under a CC0 licence). |

No organiser data, no real patient data, no scraped data, and no third-party datasets are used.

## 4. Personal data: what we keep, and why

Even though every record is invented, Jissr is built as if the data were real, and collects only what the follow-up needs.

| Field | Why it is needed |
|---|---|
| Name | To greet the patient and let staff identify the case. (A real service would identify patients through the health record, not free text.) |
| Age | Clinical context for prioritising follow-up. |
| Preferred language | To communicate in a language the patient understands. |
| Works shifts (yes/no) | To offer appointment times the patient can attend. |
| HbA1c value and date | The actionable result itself. |
| Barrier, contact channel, confidence score | Chosen by the patient, to adapt the next step and measure understanding. |
| Family helper name | Only with the patient's consent; sees appointment status, never the result. |

**Not collected:** Emirates ID or any ID number, phone number, address, nationality, sex, date of birth, medical history.

**Where data lives:** only in the user's own browser (the demo). Nothing is stored on a server. The AI request carries only the result band, the number, the language and the barrier: never a name or identifier. This is checked automatically (`tests/`, and live on `trust.html`).
