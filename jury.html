From a lab result to the patient's phone, in seconds

When a result arrives, Falaj's rules decide what kind of result it is, how fast it must go, who looks after it and how the patient is told. Agents send and chase; a person tells anything serious and makes every decision.

  2. SortedHbA1c 5.7–6.4 routine, 6.5–9.9 priority, 10+ urgent. BI-RADS 4 priority, 5 urgent. FIT positive: priority.Rules
  3. Deadlines setUrgent: told the same day, seen in 2 days. Priority: told in 1 day, seen in 14. Proposals for a clinician to confirm.Rules

  5. How to tellSerious result: a person must call, never a message alone. Otherwise a message in the patient's language and channel.Rules

  7. Watched until doneLate steps turn amber and alert the keeper. The water moves only when a person records the step.Rules + people

Example. Priya's mammogram comes back BI-RADS 4. Falaj sorts it as priority, with a 14-day deadline, and names Nurse Peter as keeper. Because it is suspicious, it cannot be sent as a text: the agent calls Priya in Malayalam, never mentions the result, holds a visit slot and asks Nurse Peter to call her today. He tells her in person. (Synthetic patient.)

Why not just a notification: in a randomised trial, automatic reminders alone did no better than usual care (22.7% vs 22.9% at 120 days), while active outreach and navigation reached 31.0% and 31.4% (Atlas et al., JAMA 2023). So Falaj does not stop at the message: an agent calls, and a person follows up.

How Falaj answers the challenge

  1. End-to-end visibility. One journey per result: told, understood, booked, seen, confirmed, referred, care plan, closed. The care team sees how many patients stopped at each step, and who.
  2. Ownership and deadlines. Every result has a named keeper from the first second. Deadlines depend on urgency; a late step turns amber and alerts the keeper.
  3. Patients reached in their language. The first message is in the patient's language with a translation, a serious result is told by a person, and the patient has an assistant that organises appointments, reminders and documents.
  4. Across providers. One Falaj Health ID and a QR code: a doctor anywhere types the ID and the name to see the history. A referral is accepted or rejected, and the sender stays responsible until it is accepted. Cases export in the standard FHIR format.
  5. People decide, and closing needs proof. Only a clinician can close a loop, with written evidence. AI is refused by the code itself, and the tests prove it.

Before Falaj, and with Falaj

Before

  • The result leaves the lab as a letter or a portal message, often in a language the patient does not read.
  • Nobody is named as responsible once it is sent.
  • A referral can wait for days without anyone noticing.
  • Each clinic sees only its own records.

With Falaj

  • Rules set an owner and a deadline in seconds; the patient is reached in their language.
  • A named keeper at every step, alerted when a step is late.
  • The sender stays responsible until a referral is accepted; an agent chases after 2 days.
  • One Health ID opens the patient's care history in any clinic.

What already exists, and what Falaj adds

Falaj did not invent closed-loop follow-up. Platforms in the US and the UK already track abnormal results (Azra AI, Inflo Health), referrals between providers (Infina Connect) and patient outreach (Luma Health); the NHS runs national screening recall. And it works: adding patient outreach raised follow-up within 120 days from about 23% to 31% (Atlas et al., JAMA 2023); bilingual phone navigation raised colonoscopy from 42.1% to 55.1% (Coronado et al., Annals of Internal Medicine 2025).

The gap in the UAE. About nine in ten residents are expatriates, speaking many languages, often working shifts, and moving between public and private providers, insurers and jobs. Tools built for one hospital network in one or two languages lose them at every hand-over. Falaj follows the patient, not the hospital.

What this prototype is not (yet)

  • Synthetic data only. All patients, clinicians, facilities ("Family Clinic A", "General Hospital C") and records are invented. Nothing real is stored; data stays in your browser.
  • Sign-in is a demo. A real service would use the national digital identity for patients and the hospital account for staff.
  • Messages and calls are simulated. The in-app call can use a natural voice; a call to a real phone number would need a telephony service. A real service needs UAE health-data compliance (DoH policy, ADHICS), Malaffi integration and patient consent.
  • Deadlines are proposals for a clinician to confirm. The FHIR export has not been checked with the official validator. Arabic text needs review by a native-speaking clinician.
  • Sources. Figures: only the two trials above. Result categories: HbA1c bands and BI-RADS categories as published by their professional bodies. Codes: LOINC (free licence). Fonts: open licences, bundled with the site; no third-party scripts.

Why the name

The aflaj carried water through connected channels to every palm garden of the oases, kept clear by the community, with a fair turn for each family. In 2020, UNESCO inscribed Al Aflaj, the UAE's traditional irrigation network, on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. The idea maps onto care: the water source is the result, the channels are the care pathways, the keeper is the named clinician, and the water reaching the palm is the patient receiving care.

Falaj (فلج) is the water channel that has fed the oases of the Emirates for centuries: every plot, a named keeper, a known turn. Falaj does the same for care.
