Why Falaj

The aflaj carried water through connected channels to every palm garden of the oases, kept clear by the community, with a fair turn for each family. In 2020, UNESCO inscribed Al Aflaj, the UAE's traditional irrigation network and the knowledge to build and maintain it, on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. The idea maps onto care, one to one:

The falajFalaj, for care
The water sourceThe screening result
The connected channelsReferrals and care pathways
Sharing the water fairlyCoordinating care, so nobody is skipped

Water reaching the oasisThe patient receiving the right care

Water sustains life. Continuity sustains care. Detecting a problem is only the beginning; making sure care follows is what makes the difference.

Before Falaj, and with Falaj

Before

  • The result leaves the lab as a letter or a portal message, often in a language the patient does not read.
  • Nobody is named as responsible once it is sent.
  • A referral can wait for days without anyone noticing.
  • Nobody can say whether the patient was ever seen.

With Falaj

  • The Intake agent reads the report; a person confirms the result; an owner and a deadline are set by rules the same day.
  • The Outreach agent calls in the patient's language; a nurse checks understanding.
  • The sender stays responsible until a referral is accepted; the Referral agent chases after 2 days.
  • The loop closes only when a doctor confirms care, and the sender is told.

The only figures we quote are from published trials: reaching patients raised follow-up from about 23% to 31% (JAMA 2023), and phone navigation in the patient's language from 42.1% to 55.1% (Annals of Internal Medicine 2025). Falaj has not been measured yet; a pilot would.

What already exists, and what Falaj adds

Falaj did not invent closed-loop follow-up. Platforms in the US and the UK already track abnormal results (Azra AI, Inflo Health), referrals between providers (Infina Connect) and patient outreach (Luma Health); the NHS runs national screening recall. And it works: in a trial of 44 primary care practices, adding patient outreach raised follow-up within 120 days from about 23% to 31% (Atlas et al., JAMA 2023); bilingual phone navigation after an abnormal bowel test raised colonoscopy from 42.1% to 55.1% (Coronado et al., Annals of Internal Medicine 2025).

The gap in the UAE. About nine in ten residents are expatriates, speaking many languages, often working shifts, and moving between public and private providers, insurers and jobs. Tools built for one US hospital network, in English and Spanish, lose them at every handover. Falaj is the care team's view of that system: every result as water flowing to care, a named keeper at every channel, reached in the patient's own language, across every organisation.

How Falaj answers the challenge

  1. End-to-end visibility. One journey per case: result, notified, understood, appointment, attended, confirmatory test, referral, treatment or surveillance plan, closed with evidence.
  2. Ownership and service levels. Every case has a named owner. Deadlines depend on urgency. Overdue, rejected and unassigned cases go to a "needs attention" queue, set by fixed escalation rules.
  3. Patient-centred communication. Plain English and Arabic, read-aloud, "not a diagnosis" every time, a check that the patient understood, a barrier check-in that changes the next step, a chosen channel, and a family helper with the patient's consent.
  4. Cross-provider coordination. Referral with accept or reject, ownership handed to the receiving clinician and back with a report, plus clinician-to-clinician questions with a reply deadline. Cases export in the standard FHIR format.
  5. Human-governed escalation and closure. Urgent and exceptional cases are flagged to clinical leads. Only a clinician can close, with written evidence. The AI can do none of this, and tests prove it.
  6. Measures. A live measures page: time to each step, referral and appointment completion, unresolved results, reasons for delay, patient confidence, with a pilot plan for proving real improvement.

If it fails–A pre-written message is used, so nothing breaks
  • Every patient message ends with “This is not a diagnosis.” The code adds it, so it cannot be left out.
  • AI text passes a safety filter first (no “you have diabetes”, no medicine advice, right language). If it fails, the pre-written text is shown.
  • The AI is sent only a band, a number and a barrier. It never receives a name or any patient detail.
  • A case closes only when a clinician gives a reason and written evidence. The rule is enforced in code, not just by a greyed-out button, and it is tested.
  • The AI provider sits behind one adapter (Gemini, Anthropic or Azure OpenAI), so moving to Azure OpenAI is a settings change.

What this prototype is not (yet)

  • All 30 patients are invented. Nothing real is stored; data stays in your browser. Every data source and licence is declared on the safety page.
  • The staff role switch is a demo. A real system would use proper sign-in (for example Microsoft Entra ID).
  • Reminders, messages and calls are simulated. A real service would need UAE health-data compliance (DoH policy, ADHICS), Malaffi integration and patient consent management.
  • Follow-up windows and service levels are proposals for a clinician to confirm. The FHIR export has not been checked with the official validator.
  • Arabic text still needs review by a native-speaking clinician.
